Personal Development

Reflection on The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason

Introduction

After reading "Seven Cures for a Lean Purse" and "The Five Laws of Gold" from George S. Clason's The Richest Man in Babylon, I gained a deeper understanding of how important it is to manage money wisely. The chapters taught me that financial stability is not just about how much money a person receives, but also about how that money is saved and spent. As a university student who mostly depends on monthly allowances, I found the First and Second Cures most applicable to my life. They encouraged me to reflect on my spending habits and recognize that even the smallest financial decisions can have a significant impact over time.

First Cure: Start Thy Purse to Fattening

The first cure emphasizes the importance of saving a portion of your income before spending the remainder. Clason encourages us to keep one-tenth of our earnings, explaining, "For each ten coins I put in, to spend but nine" (Clason, 1926, p.28). This means that we should save at least one-tenth of our earnings before spending the rest. I find this principle very meaningful because, although I depend on an allowance and sometimes find it difficult to save, I realize that I do not need to wait until I have a larger income to develop good spending habits. I can practice to set aside not necessarily one-tenth each month, but an amount that is manageable enough. This would help me build discipline and gradually work towards becoming a person of financial independence.

Second Cure: Control Thy Expenditures

The second cure focuses on controlling our spending and telling the difference between our needs and wants. Clason (1926) states, "Confuse not the necessary expenses with thy desires" (p.29). This made me think about how at times I purchase little things, such as snacks or small accessories, without fully considering their impact on my finances. Although these purchases may seem insignificant in the short term, often times they add up and could contribute towards transportation costs or other essential expenses. When I realize this, at times I feel frustrated because I recognize that I could have spent my money more wisely.

First Law of Gold

Having read the Five Laws of God, the first one stuck with me the most as it reinforces the lessons learned from the previous cures. It emphasizes the importance of consistently saving a portion of our earnings. Clason (1926) explains that gold comes to those who "put by not less than one-tenth of his earnings" (p.70). This connects with the First Cure as both encourages us to save before spending, whereas the Second Cure shows us why the need controlling unnecessary expenses makes this possible. Together, these lessons have helped me understand that saving and budgeting are essential for achieving financial stability.

My Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from these chapters is that good financial habits should begin now, regardless of how much money we have. Before reading, I knew saving was important, but I have not always considered how my everyday spending choices have impacted my ability to save and invest for the future. Now I understand that building financial responsibilty requires discipline, planning and consistency. Moving forward, I will strive to be more mindful of my spending, distinguish between my need and wants, and make saving a more regular part of managing my allowance. By applying these lessons, I hope to develop strong financial skills that will help me achieve my goal of becoming a person of ultimate financial independence.
