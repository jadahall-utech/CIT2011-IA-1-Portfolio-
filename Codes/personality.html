Personality

My Top Three Hobbies

Listening to Music, Singing, and Dancing

Listening to music, singing, and dancing keep me calm and help me relieve stress. Some days after getting home from school, I feel either very drained, overstimulated, or just not in the mood, and these are the things I turn to. They lift my spirits and bring me out of that state, especially when all are done at once. Once I feel settled again, it's much easier for me to sit and focus on studying and completing assignments.

Crossword Puzzles

Doing crossword puzzles strengthens the problem-solving skills I rely on when coding. Working through them teaches me to stay patient, find ways to break down complex problems, and keep trying different approaches until I find a solution. That mindset is invaluable when debugging code or designing efficient algorithms.

Time with Friends

For me, spending time with my friends is something I value, as it gives me a break from the pressure of keeping up with schoolwork and other responsibilities. Being around the people I care about keeps me motivated and reminds me that I don't have to work through life alone. Many times after leaving a class session, I hang out with friends to unwind and to keep my mind on track.

My Hero

One person that has been a significant source of motivation for me is my high school dance teacher. She has always been a joy to be around. In about grade 8 or 9, during a rehearsal, she told me that I was dancing within myself. Thinking about that statement in retrospect, she was right. When I started dancing in high school, I wasn't the most confident, and I held back with realizing it. Rather than letting me stay comfortable, she began putting me in pieces I wasn't very comfortable with in order to push me out of my comfort zone. In doing so, she helped me grow and become a better dancer. She once made me the lead in a dance, and I held my own and did well.

The biggest challenge was a JCDC folk dance that was fairly difficult, and there were moments where I would even cry after coming off the stage. During that period, I questioned my abilities and seriously considered giving up. However, with the support and encouragement of my dance teacher, I persevered and ultimately overcame the challenge. In the end, we won both the parish finals and national championships.

She is my hero because she saw more in me than I saw in myself and pushed me to reach my full potential. Not only has this impacted my dancing, but it has also shaped my approach to challenges in other areas of my life.

LLMs as a Tool, Not a Crutch: My Take as a Student

Using LLMs Effectively

Let's be honest: ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot have become a part of student life almost overnight. But here's the question I constantly ask myself: are these tools helping me learn, or are they making me lazy? I think the answer lies in how we use them. If we rely on them to do our thinking for us, we risk losing our ability to think critically and solve problems independently. But if we use them as a resource to deepen our understanding and creativity, they can be incredibly powerful.

Tool vs. Crutch

In my view, using an LLM as a tool helps me better understand concepts so I can do my assignments on my own, whereas using it as a crutch makes me overly dependent and does the work for me. As a Computer Science student, this difference shows up in my approach to debugging and problem-solving. When used as a tool, an LLM explains what an error is and how to fix it, while a it as a crutch takes my broken code and hands the fixed version to me.

My Approach

Personally, I try my very best to not use LLMs to hand in my work. I use them to understand material better and to generate ideas for my own work. Here's what that looks like:

  • When: when I am stuck on a concept, when an error message isn't familiar or doesn't make sense to me, and when I am preparing for exams or working on projects that require creative thinking.
  • Where: in my personal study sessions and during collaborative work with peers or when reading documentation.
  • How: by asking it to explain or clarify ideas, break down complex problems, and generate questions to test my understanding.

The Threshold of Dependency

The biggest risk for me is becoming too dependent on LLMs. If an LLM finds and fixes every bug, eventually I might lose the ability to work through problems independently, and that is very much one of the most needed skills as a developer. Hu (2024) warns that treating LLMs as if they are search engines for instant answers to our questions can bypass thinking critically and solving problems on our own. Furthermore, UNESCO's guidance similarly emphasizes a human-centered approach that prioritizes human agency in how these tools are made use of (Miao & Holmes, 2023). Both sources point to the very same idea: the thinking has to stay with us.

The Step Forward

From now on, I ask for explanations and guidance, not answers. Hu (2024) also encourages students to question the information given to us by LLMs. With that being said, I will make it best practice to check their responses for accuracy and logic before trusting them. You should too!
